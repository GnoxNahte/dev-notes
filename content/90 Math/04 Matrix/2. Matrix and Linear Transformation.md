## Definition of Matrix Transformations
### Meaning of each column in matrix - ==IMPT==!
Using a 2x2 matrix as example,
$$
M=\begin{bmatrix}
a & b  \\
c & d
\end{bmatrix}
$$
- $\begin{bmatrix}a\\ c\end{bmatrix}$ represents the result of applying the matrix on a unit vector along the **x-axis** (commonly shown as $\hat{i}$, which is ($\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$)
- $\begin{bmatrix}b\\ d\end{bmatrix}$ represents the result of applying the matrix on a unit vector along the **y-axis** (commonly shown as $\hat{j}$, which is ($\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$)

Why is this true?
- $M\hat{i} = \begin{bmatrix}a & b \\ c & d\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}1 \\0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}a\\b\end{bmatrix}$ 
- $M\hat{j} = \begin{bmatrix}a & b \\ c & d\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}0 \\1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}b\\d\end{bmatrix}$

> [!NOTE] Notation when chaining matrix multiplications
> The order is right to left
> For example,
> Given $M_{R}M_{S}\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$ where $M_{R}$ and $M_{S}$ are 2 matrices, and $\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$ is the vector/point to be transformed,
> $M_{S}\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$ will be done first, then the result of that will be multiplied with $M_{R}$
> So, something like this: $M_{R}(M_{S}\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix})$
> 
> This notation is from [[90 Math/00 Basics/Functions - f(x)#Composite Function\|Function notation]]

## Transformation Types
### Summary

| Type                 | Matrix                                                                                        | Inverse Matrix (reverse transformation)                                                       | Relation between matrix and it's inverse                                                       |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Projection           | $M=\frac{1}{\|\vec{d}\|^2}\vec{d}\ \vec{d^T}$                                                 | Doesn't exist                                                                                 | Inverse doesn't exist <br>as Projection compresses all points onto 1 line, losing information. |
| Reflection           | $M=\frac{2}{\|\vec{d}\|^2}\vec{d}\ \vec{d^T}-I$                                               | $M=\frac{2}{\|\vec{d}\|^2}\vec{d}\ \vec{d^T}-I$                                               | Inverse is the same matrix as it reflects back on the same line                                |
| Scaling              | $M = \begin{bmatrix}a & 0 \\ 0 & b\end{bmatrix}$                                              | $M = \begin{bmatrix}a^{-1} & 0 \\ 0 & b^{-1}\end{bmatrix}$                                    |                                                                                                |
| Rotation             | $M = \begin{bmatrix}\cos{\theta} & -\sin{\theta} \\ \sin{\theta} & \cos{\theta}\end{bmatrix}$ | $M = \begin{bmatrix}\cos{\theta} & \sin{\theta} \\ -\sin{\theta} & \cos{\theta}\end{bmatrix}$ | Matrix rotates anti-clockwise,<br>Inverse matrix rotate clockwise                              |
| Shear (along axis)   | $M = \begin{bmatrix}1 & a \\ b & 1\end{bmatrix}$<br>                                          | $M = \begin{bmatrix}1 & -a \\ -b & 1\end{bmatrix}$                                            |                                                                                                |
| Shear (along a line) | $M = I + \frac{1}{\|\vec{n}\|}\vec{v}\ \vec{n}^T$                                             | $M = I - \frac{1}{\|\vec{n}\|}\vec{v}\ \vec{n}^T$                                             |                                                                                                |

### Projection onto a line ([[90 Math/04 Matrix/2E. Matrix Linear Transformation\|Info]])

$$
M
= \frac{1}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d}\ \vec{d}\ ^T
=\frac{1}{||\vec{d}||^2}\begin{bmatrix}
d_{x}^2 & d_{x}d_{y} \\
d_{x}d_{y} & d_{y}^2
\end{bmatrix}
$$

### Reflection ([[90 Math/04 Matrix/2E. Matrix Linear Transformation#Reflection\|Info]])
$$
M= \frac{2}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d}\ \vec{d}\ ^T - I
$$

### Scale
$$
M = \begin{bmatrix}
a & 0 \\
0 & b
\end{bmatrix}
$$
- a scales on x-axis
- b scales on y-axis

Features
- Changes area
- Keeps shape
- This is opposite of [[90 Math/04 Matrix/2. Matrix and Linear Transformation#Shear]]

### Shear (along axis)
$$
M = \begin{bmatrix}
1 & a \\
b & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Where:
- $a$ shears on y-axis
- b shears on x-axis

Features
- Keeps area
- Changes shape
- This is opposite of [[90 Math/04 Matrix/2. Matrix and Linear Transformation#Scale]] 

### Shear (along line)
$$
M = I + \frac{1}{||\vec{n}||}\vec{v}\ \vec{n}^T
$$

Where:
- $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{n}$ represent the vector and normal equation of the line respectively 

Features
- Keeps area (? - #todo check)
- Changes shape
- This is opposite of [[90 Math/04 Matrix/2. Matrix and Linear Transformation#Scale]]
