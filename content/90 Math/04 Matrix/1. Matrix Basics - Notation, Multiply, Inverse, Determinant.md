### Multiplication Laws

| Law                       | Example                            | Note                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Multiply by **Identity**  | $IA = A$                           |                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Multiply by **Zero**      | $A0 = 0$                           |                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Multiply by **Inverse**   | $AA^{-1}=A^{-1}A=I$                |                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
|                           | $(AB)^{-1}=B^{-1}A^{-1}$           |                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Multiply by **Transpose** | $A^TA$                             | Doesn't result in anything special, but it'll be symmetric                                                                                                                                             |
| **Non-commutativity**     | $AB\neq BA$                        | Very important!                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Associativity**         | $(AB)C = A(BC)$                    | As long as the size of all 3 matrices are allowed are to be multiplied<br><br>Also means can multiply matrices both ways: (Left -> Right) or (Right <- Left)<br>Just cannot swap the order $AB\neq BA$ |
| **Unable to cancel**      | $AB=AC \;\not\!\!\!\implies B = C$ |                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Multiply by               |                                    |                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

> [!WARNING] Multiply by **Zero** and Multiply by **Inverse**
> If $AB = 0$, it does NOT mean that A = 0 or B = 0
> as B can be a inverse of A and result in 0

## Matrix multiplication

See also [[#Multiplication Laws]]

> [!NOTE] Can multiply matrices both ways: (Left -> Right) or (Right <- Left)
> As matrix multiplication is Associative: $(AB)C = A(BC)$
> Just cannot swap the order $AB\neq BA$
>
> However, note that when applying matrix to vectors, it applies Right -> Left
> For example, when computing the [[00 - 2D Matrix Transformation Overview#Model to World|Model -> World]] transformation,
> Order is apply: Scale -> Rotate -> Translate, but the final matrix is $M = M_T \cdot M_R \cdot M_S$
>
> Working of it:
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \vec{v_2}&=(M_T \ (M_R \ (M_S \cdot \vec{v_1}))) \\
> &=(M_T \cdot M_R \cdot M_S) \vec{v_1}
> \end{align}
> $$

## Matrix Transpose

Swap the elements along the main diagonal
![[90 Math/04 Matrix/Drawings/Matrix.excalidraw.md#^frame=_D8IRn3ceX791sP2IOWJs|800]]

```tabs
--- 2x2 Matrix

$$
M = \begin{bmatrix}
a & b \\
c & d
\end{bmatrix} \to \ M^T = \begin{bmatrix}
a & c \\
b & d
\end{bmatrix}
$$

`a` and `d` are the main diagonals, swap along it so
`b` and `c` are swapped

--- 3x3 Matrix
$$
M = \begin{bmatrix}
a & b & c \\
d & e & f \\
g & h & i
\end{bmatrix} \to \ M^T = \begin{bmatrix}
a & d & g \\
b & e & h  \\
c & f & i
\end{bmatrix}
$$

`a`, `e`, `i` are the main diaongals, swap every cell along it

--- 3x1 Matrix (or vector)
Example with non-square matrix:
$$
M=\begin{bmatrix}
x \\
y \\
z
\end{bmatrix} \to M^T = \begin{bmatrix}
x & y & z
\end{bmatrix}
$$
The size will change (3x1 matrix -> 1x3 matrix), imagine swapping along an imaginary diagonal
```

## Matrix Inverse

This is matrix's version of Divide

```tabs
--- 2x2 Matrix
Formula:
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
a & b \\
c & d
\end{bmatrix}^{-1}
=
\frac{1}{ad-bc}
\begin{bmatrix}
d & -b \\
-c & a
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Steps:
- Calculate scalar: $\frac{1}{\text{determinant}}$ 
- Swap main diagonals
- Negate anti-diagonals

> [!NOTE] $ad-bc$ is the [[#Determinant]]
> Since it's dividing by the determinant, the determinant **cannot** be 0 (as cannot divide by 0)
> Related: [[#Checking if the Matrix has a Inverse]]

Source: [Math is Fun](https://www.mathsisfun.com/algebra/matrix-inverse.html) (Scroll down to "2x2 Matrix")
--- 3x3 Matrix
Simplest to do by hand: Use [Gauss jordan](https://www.mathsisfun.com/algebra/matrix-inverse-row-operations-gauss-jordan.html) method
This is not the most efficient method for programming though. (Just use libraries to make it easier)

--- 3x3 Affine Matrix
This is a special case

Let
$$
M=\begin{bmatrix}
a & b & c \\
d & e & f \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Splitting it into a ($2 \times 2$ matrix) $\cdot M_{\text{translation}}$

$$
M = 
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & c \\
0 & 0 & f \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}
a & b & 0 \\
d & e & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then getting the inverse of the $2 \times 2$ matrix
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
a & b \\
d & e
\end{bmatrix}^{-1}
=
\frac{1}{ae-bd}
\begin{bmatrix}
e & -b \\
-d & a
\end{bmatrix}
$$
Then inverse $M_{\text{translation}}$ and sub in the inverse of the $2 \times 2$ matrix

```

## Determinant

### Formula

#### For 2D

$$
\det\begin{bmatrix}
a & b \\
c & d
\end{bmatrix} = 
ab-cd
$$

#### For 3D

$$
\det\begin{bmatrix}
a & b & c \\
d & e & f \\
g & h & i
\end{bmatrix}  = (aei + dhc + bfg) - (gec + bdi + fha)
$$

### Using Determinant to calculate area of parallelogram

Given $\vec{u}= \begin{bmatrix}a_{1} \\ b_{1}\end{bmatrix}$ and $\vec{v}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{2}\\b_{2}\end{bmatrix}$,
Steps:

1. Put the 2 vectors side by side in a matrix
2. Area of parallelogram = the **absolute** of the **determinant** of the matrix in step 1

$$
\begin{align}
\text{Area of parallelogram} &= ||\det{\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&a_{2}\\b_{1}&b_{2}\end{bmatrix}}||  \\
&= ||a_{1}b_{2}-a_{2}b_{1}|| 
\end{align}
$$

This^ works in 3D too. To check, another method to find area is using [[3. Vectors - 3D Vectors, Lines, Planes#Finding area of triangle with cross product|cross product]] (but cross product can only work in 3D)
