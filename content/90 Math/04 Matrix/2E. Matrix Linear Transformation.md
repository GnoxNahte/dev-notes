## Notation

- $v'$ OR _image of v_ = Result of applying the matrix $M$ on vector $\vec{v}$
- $\hat{i} = \begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}$  (unit vector on x-axis)
- $\hat{j}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}$ (unit vector on y-axis)
- $M = \begin{bmatrix}\vec{i} & \vec{j}\end{bmatrix} =\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0\\0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ - (i and j represent the 1st and 2nd column of the matrix, and when using i and j, it also represents the [[1. Matrix Basics - Notation, Multiply, Inverse, Determinant#Common matrices|Identity Matrix]]) (See [3bluebrown video - @3:45 to 8:00](https://youtu.be/kYB8IZa5AuE?t=229) for a proper explanation).

## Transformation Types

### Orthogonal projection onto a line

#### Diagram

![[90 Math/04 Matrix/Drawings/Matrix.excalidraw.md#^frame=HcSo3O5tarwBMJrJH-PeJ|500]]

#### Formula

$$
M=\frac{1}{||\vec{d}||^2}\begin{bmatrix}
d_{x}^2 & d_{x}d_{y} \\
d_{x}d_{y} & d_{y}^2
\end{bmatrix}
= \frac{1}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d}\ \vec{d}\ ^T
$$

#### Explanation

Given a line ($l$) with $\vec{d}=\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}$ with origin 0 ([[2. Matrix and Linear Transformation#Definition of **Linear**|Linear transformation requires transformation around the origin]]),
Find the Matrix of any point onto the line ($l$)

Steps:

1. [[1. Vector - Basics (2D)#Vector Projection ( 1E. Vector - Basics Explanation (2D) Vector projection explanation Info )|Project]] $\vec{i}$ and $\vec{j}$ onto $l$
2. Find the matrix by using the image (result of projection/transformation)

##### Step 1 - Project $\vec{i}$ and $\vec{j}$ onto $l$

$$
\begin{align}
\vec{d}=\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix},\ \ \ \ \ &\vec{i}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix},\ \ \ \ \ \vec{j}=\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
i' &= proj_{\vec{d}}{\vec{i}} \\
&=\frac{\vec{i}\cdot \vec{d}}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d}  \\
&= \frac{\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}\cdot \begin{bmatrix}d_{x}\\d_{y}\end{bmatrix}}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d} \\
&= \frac{d_{x}}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d}\\
\end{align}
$$

Same for $j'$ except replace $d_{x}$ with $d_{y}$

$$
j'=\frac{d_{y}}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d}
$$

Take out common factors out of $i'$ and $j'$

$$
\begin{align}
i'&=\frac{d_x}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d} \\
&=\frac{d_{x}}{||\vec{d}||^2}\begin{bmatrix}d_{x}\\ d_{y}\end{bmatrix} \\
&=\frac{1}{||\vec{d}||^2}\begin{bmatrix}d_{x}^2\\ d_{y}\end{bmatrix} \\
\end{align}
$$

Similar for $j'$,

$$
j'=\frac{1}{||\vec{d}||^2}\begin{bmatrix}d_{x}\\ d_{y}^2\end{bmatrix}
$$

##### Step 2 - Find the matrix by using $i'$ and $j'$

$$
M=\frac{1}{||\vec{d}||^2}\begin{bmatrix}
d_{x}^2 & d_{x}d_{y} \\
d_{x}d_{y} & d_{y}^2
\end{bmatrix}
$$

See [[2. Matrix and Linear Transformation#Meaning of each column in matrix - IMPT!|why this works]]

##### Step 3 (optional) - Simplify it

$$
\begin{align}

M&=\frac{1}{||\vec{d}||^2}\begin{bmatrix}
d_{x}^2 & d_{x}d_{y} \\
d_{x}d_{y} & d_{y}^2
\end{bmatrix}\\
 &= \frac{1}{||\vec{d}||^2}
\begin{bmatrix}dx & dy\end{bmatrix} 
\begin{bmatrix}dx \\ dy\end{bmatrix} \\
 &= \frac{1}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d}\ \vec{d}\ ^T
\end{align}
$$

### Reflection

![[90 Math/04 Matrix/Drawings/Matrix.excalidraw.md#^frame=9I487vJ-TqsqlHa1wte2y|500]]

#### Formula

$$
M= \frac{2}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d}\ \vec{d}\ ^T - I
$$

#### Explanation

Given a line with $\vec{d}=\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\end{bmatrix}$ with origin 0 ([[2. Matrix and Linear Transformation#Definition of **Linear**|Linear transformation requires transformation around the origin)]]
Steps:

1. Find $i''$ by using [[1. Vector - Basics (2D)#Mid point|Midpoint]] formula ($i'$ as the midpoint, $\hat{i}$ as initial point, and $i''$ as the other reflected point)
2. Combine into 1 matrix and simplify it

##### Step 1 - Find $i''$ by using [[1. Vector - Basics (2D)#Mid point|Midpoint formula]]

Let $i'$ be the midpoint,
To find a point using

1. midpoint and
2. another point

$$
\begin{align}
&\text{Midpoint of P and Q} =\frac{P + Q}{2}\\
&Q = 2\times \text{Midpoint} - P
\end{align}
$$

So, to find $i''$,

$$
i'' = 2i' - \hat{i}
$$

Same for $j''$,

$$
j'' = 2j' - \hat{j}
$$

##### Step 2 - Combine and simplify

Combine using [[2. Matrix and Linear Transformation#Meaning of each column in matrix - IMPT!|i and j representation]]

> [!NOTE] Use short-form format to represent matrices
> The vector represents the whole column
>
> $$
> \text{Example matrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\vec{a} & \vec{b}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}a_{x} & b_{x} \\ a_{y} & b_{y}\end{bmatrix}
> $$

$$
\begin{align}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}i'' & j''\end{bmatrix} \\
&=\begin{bmatrix}2i'-\hat{i} & 2j'-\hat{j}\end{bmatrix} \\
&=\begin{bmatrix}2i' & 2j'\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}\hat{i} & \hat{j}\end{bmatrix} \\
\end{align}
$$

Sub $\begin{bmatrix}i' & j'\end{bmatrix}$ with [[2. Matrix and Linear Transformation#Orthogonal projection onto a line ( 2E. Matrix Linear Transformation Info )|Projection onto line formula]],

$$
M = \frac{2}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d}\ \vec{d}\ ^T - \begin{bmatrix}\hat{i} & \hat{j}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Sub $\begin{bmatrix}\hat{i} & \hat{j}\end{bmatrix}$ with $I$ ([[1. Matrix Basics - Notation, Multiply, Inverse, Determinant#Common matrices|Identity Matrix]])

> [!NOTE] Why it works?
>
> $$
> \begin{bmatrix}\hat{i} & \hat{j}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}
> $$

$$
M= \frac{2}{||\vec{d}||^2}\vec{d}\ \vec{d}\ ^T - I
$$
