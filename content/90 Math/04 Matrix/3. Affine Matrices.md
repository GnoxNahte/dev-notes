## Formula

$$
T(\vec{x})=A(\vec{x}-\vec{x_{0}})+\vec{x_{0}}
$$

Three steps:

- $(\vec{x}-\vec{x_{0}})$ - translate to origin
- $A(\vec{x}-\vec{x_{0}})$ - Applies [[2. Matrix and Linear Transformation|Linear Transformation]]
- $+\vec{x_{0}}$ - Shifts back to original position

Where:

- $\vec{x_{0}}$ is the translation to the center $(0,0)$ <span style="opacity: 0.6">(If transforming a line/plane, can use any point on the line/plane)</span>
- $A$ is the [[2. Matrix and Linear Transformation|linear transformation matrix]]

### Representing the formula into an Affine Matrix

From the formula above, expand it

$$
\begin{align}
T(\vec{x})&=A(\vec{x}-\vec{x_{0}})+\vec{x_{0}} \\
&=A\vec{x} - A\vec{x}_{0} + \vec{x}_{0}
\end{align}
$$

So,

- $A\vec{x}$ - represents the [[2. Matrix and Linear Transformation|Linear Transformation]] matrix
- $-A\vec{x}_{0}+\vec{x}_{0}$ - represents a vector to shift the result

With actual values (instead of variables), simplify the vector ($-A\vec{x}_{0}+\vec{x}_{0}$) into a vector represented as $\vec{b}$:

$$
T(\vec{x}) = A\vec{x} + \vec{b}
$$

Which can be split into

$$
T(\vec{x}) = \begin{bmatrix}
a & b \\ c & d
\end{bmatrix}\vec{x} + \begin{bmatrix}
b_{x} \\ b_{y}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

It can be represented in a 2x3 matrix:

$$
A = \begin{bmatrix}
a & b & b_x \\
c & d & b_y
\end{bmatrix}
$$

An example when it's applied to a vector:

$$
\begin{align}

A &= \begin{bmatrix}
a & b & b_x \\
c & d & b_y
\end{bmatrix}, 

\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix}
v_x \\ v_y
\end{bmatrix} \\ 

A\vec{v} &= \begin{bmatrix}
a & b & b_x \\
c & d & b_y
\end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix}
v_x \\ v_y
\end{bmatrix} \\

&=
\end{align}
$$
