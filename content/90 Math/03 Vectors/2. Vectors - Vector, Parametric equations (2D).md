## Linear equation
For reference, this is the normal way to represent an equation
$$
y=mx+c
$$
Where 
- $m$ = slope and
- $c$ = y-intercept
### Find using $\vec{n}$ and 1 point ([[90 Math/03 Vectors/2E. Vector - Vector, Parametric equations Explanation (2D)#Proof - Find using $ vec{n}$ and 1 point\|Proof]]) 
$$\vec{x}\cdot \vec{n}=\vec{OP}\cdot \vec{n}$$
Expanded out:
$$
ax+by=ax_{0}+by_{0}
$$

## Vector equation
### Notation
Let Line ($l$) be a line through $P=(x_{0}, y_{0})$ with a direction $\vec{d}=\begin{bmatrix}a\\b\end{bmatrix}$
$$\vec{x}=P+t\vec{d}\ \ \ \ \ OR \ \ \ \ \ \vec{x}=(x_{0}, y_{0})+\begin{bmatrix}a\\b\end{bmatrix}$$
> [!NOTE] $\vec{x}$ represents the line (or plane) equation
> $\vec{x}=(x,y)$
> It's a bit weird but textbooks and online sources use it

> [!EXAMPLE]-
> ```tabs
> --- Question
> Line $l$ goes through $P=(1,2)$ and has direction $\vec{d}=\begin{bmatrix}2\\5\end{bmatrix}$.
> Find the *vector equation* and *3 points* on $l$
> --- Answer
> Find vector equation
> $$\begin{align}
> \vec{x}&=P+t\vec{d} \\
> \vec{x}&=(1,2)+t\begin{bmatrix}2\\5\end{bmatrix} \\
> \end{align}$$
> Find 3 points 
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \text{Sub t=0,} \\
> (x,y) &= (1,2) \\ \\
> \text{Sub t=1,} \\
> (x,y) &= (1,2)+\begin{bmatrix}2\\5\end{bmatrix} \\
> &=(3,7)\\
> \text{Sub t=2,}  \\
> (x,y) &= (1,2)+2\begin{bmatrix}2\\5\end{bmatrix} \\
> &=(5,12)
> \end{align}
> $$
>```

### Find vector equation using 2 points
$l$ goes through $P=(0,3)$ and $Q=(9,0)$. Find the vector equation.
Steps:
1. Find out the direction vector ($\vec{PQ}$)
2. $\vec{x}=P+\vec{PQ}$ 

Working:
$\vec{PQ}=\begin{bmatrix}9\\-3\end{bmatrix}$ 
$\vec{x}=(0,3)+t\begin{bmatrix}9\\-3\end{bmatrix}$ 

### Linear $\to$ Vector equation
Given $3x+4y=5$, what is the vector equation?
Steps:
1. Find out $P$
2. Find out $\vec{d}$

Step 1 (Find $P$):
$$
\begin{align}
\text{Sub}\ x=0,
4y=5 \\
y=\frac{5}{4}
\end{align}
$$
So $P=\left( 0, \frac{5}{4} \right)$
Step 2 (Find $\vec{d}$):
A line $ax+by=c$ has the normal vector $\begin{bmatrix}a\\b\end{bmatrix}$
So, $\vec{n}=\begin{bmatrix}3\\4\end{bmatrix}\implies \vec{d}=\begin{bmatrix}-4\\3\end{bmatrix}$

ANS: $\vec{x}=\left( 0, \frac{5}{4} \right)+t\begin{bmatrix}-4\\3\end{bmatrix}$

## Parametric equation
### Notation
Let Line ($l$) be a line through $P=(x_{0}, y_{0})$ with a direction $\vec{d}=\begin{bmatrix}a\\b\end{bmatrix}$
$$
\begin{cases}
x=x_{0}+ta \\
y=y_{0}+tb
\end{cases}
\ \ \ \ \ \
t\in(-\infty, \infty)
$$
This is basically [[90 Math/03 Vectors/2. Vectors - Vector, Parametric equations (2D)#Vector equation\|vector equation]] expanded out

## Finding shortest distance

### Point to line

\text{Dist from Q to line} =||proj_{\vec{n}}{\vec{PQ}}||
